# Codex Project Brief: Columbus Ballot Issues 8 & 9 Infographic

## Objective

Create a polished, nonpartisan, voter-friendly infographic explaining **Columbus, Ohio Ballot Issues 8 and 9** for the **November 3, 2026 General Election**.

The infographic should help voters understand the proposals, their differences, their origins, and the consequences of each possible voting combination.

**Primary goal:** A voter should understand the essential differences and voting outcomes in **30 seconds or less**, without having to read lengthy ballot language.

Produce a professional, visually engaging, shareable graphic suitable for social media, mobile screens, and printing. Prioritize clarity, factual accuracy, and accessibility.

## Audience and communication priorities

Target ordinary Columbus voters who may be unfamiliar with the current City Council election system.

Answer these questions in order of importance:

1. What changes under Issue 8 versus Issue 9?
2. What happens if both pass?
3. How does the current system work?
4. Why are there two similar proposals?
5. What are the tradeoffs between district and citywide representation?

**Lead with the practical differences, not the political history.** The election-outcome matrix should be the visual centerpiece, alongside a prominent comparison of 9 versus 13 council seats.

## Required infographic copy

Use the following as the primary editorial copy. You may tighten phrasing to fit the design, but preserve factual meaning, neutrality, and all essential information.

---

# TWO ISSUES. ONE CITY COUNCIL.
### Columbus Ballot Issues 8 & 9 — Explained

**Both proposals change how Columbus elects its City Council. But they don't create the same council.**

### HOW IT WORKS TODAY

**9 districts. 9 council members. Everyone votes for all 9.**

Each council member must live in the district they represent, but every Columbus voter can vote in every council race.

### WHAT WOULD CHANGE?

#### ISSUE 8 — District Representation
*Proposed by residents through Our City, Our Say*

**9 seats. 9 districts. No citywide council seats.**

- Your district elects its own representative.
- You vote in one council district race.
- Council stays at 9 members.
- Existing district boundaries remain until the regular redistricting cycle.

#### ISSUE 9 — District + Citywide Representation
*Proposed by Columbus City Council*

**13 seats. 9 district representatives + 4 citywide representatives.**

- Your district elects its own representative.
- Every voter also elects four at-large council members.
- Council grows from 9 to 13 members, phased in through 2030.
- District boundaries are redrawn earlier, in 2028.

**Important:** At-large members don't supervise district members. All council members have equal legislative voting authority.

### WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU VOTE...

| Issue 8 | Issue 9 | Result |
|---|---|---|
| NO | NO | Keep 9 members, all elected citywide |
| YES | NO | 9 members, each elected by their district |
| NO | YES | 9 district members + 4 elected citywide |
| YES | YES | 9 district members + 4 elected citywide |

**YES on both? Both amendments take effect. The resulting council has 13 members: 9 district-elected and 4 citywide-elected.**

### WHY ARE THERE TWO ISSUES?

In 2025, District 7 voters favored one candidate, but voters citywide elected another.

That raised a question:

**Should residents choose their own district representative, or should every Columbus voter have a say in all council seats?**

Our City, Our Say proposed Issue 8. Following unsuccessful compromise negotiations, City Council proposed Issue 9 to add citywide representation alongside district elections.

### THE TRADEOFF

**District representation** connects council elections directly to neighborhood voters.

**Citywide representation** gives every voter a voice in choosing members responsible for decisions affecting all of Columbus.

Neither structure guarantees better government.

**The choice is about who elects whom—and to whom elected officials are accountable.**

---

*November 3, 2026 General Election · City of Columbus, Ohio*

*Nonpartisan voter information. This guide does not endorse either proposal.*

## Design and visual hierarchy

Build a coherent infographic, not a wall of text. The graphic should communicate most of its essential information through layout, diagrams, numbers, and short labels.

### 1. Header

Use the title:

**TWO ISSUES. ONE CITY COUNCIL.**

Subtitle: **Columbus Ballot Issues 8 & 9 — Explained**

Keep the header visually strong but compact.

### 2. Current-system snapshot

Illustrate nine council seats with nine representative icons.

Show that all nine are elected citywide, despite council members residing in separate districts.

Keep the accompanying explanation brief.

### 3. Side-by-side proposal comparison

Make this one of the two strongest visual sections.

**Issue 8:**

Show nine district-representative icons, all using the same district color.

Caption: **9 district seats**

**Issue 9:**

Show nine district-representative icons plus four distinct at-large icons.

Caption: **9 district seats + 4 citywide seats**

Use different colors or icon treatments for district and at-large representatives, supported by text labels rather than relying on color alone.

Do not portray at-large representatives as supervisors, managers, or authorities over district representatives.

They are legislative peers.

### 4. Voting-outcome matrix

This is the infographic's other major centerpiece.

Present the four possible YES/NO combinations using a clear, four-row matrix.

The matrix must communicate that:

- NO / NO preserves the existing system.
- YES / NO produces nine district-elected seats.
- NO / YES produces nine district seats plus four at-large seats.
- YES / YES also produces nine district seats plus four at-large seats.

Emphasize that **both issues can pass simultaneously**.

Do not imply that one amendment automatically defeats the other, or that the measure receiving more votes takes precedence.

### 5. Why two proposals exist

Use a short narrative or mini-timeline:

**2025:** District 7 election highlights disagreement between district-level and citywide voter preferences.

**2026:** Our City, Our Say advances citizen-led Issue 8.

**2026:** After unsuccessful negotiations, City Council proposes Issue 9 with additional structural reforms.

Avoid portraying either group as acting in bad faith.

Do not speculate about undisclosed negotiation motives.

### 6. Representation tradeoff

Create two balanced visual treatments:

**Neighborhood accountability:** District residents elect their representative.

**Citywide accountability:** Voters retain a voice in electing representatives with citywide constituencies.

Examples of cross-district concerns include transit, housing, infrastructure, and public services.

Do not suggest that at-large members inherently govern more effectively or that district members cannot consider citywide needs.

### 7. Closing takeaway

Use this as the final prominent sentence:

**The choice is about who elects whom—and to whom elected officials are accountable.**

This should feel like an invitation to informed voting, not a voting recommendation.

## Additional context for the designer

Columbus covers approximately 227 square miles and currently has nine residential council districts. The average district therefore represents approximately one-ninth of the city's area, although actual geographic sizes vary.

The existing system is a hybrid at-large arrangement: councilors reside in particular districts, but every Columbus voter participates in electing all nine.

A useful optional visual is an accurate map of the nine districts to reinforce the geographic structure.

Official district-map source:

https://www.columbus.gov/Government/City-Council/Council-Residential-Districts

Use the actual map only if it can be reproduced accurately and legibly. Do not create fictional district boundaries.

### Political and historical context

Issue 8 originated with the citizen-led **Our City, Our Say** coalition.

Issue 9 originated with Columbus City Council.

Reported negotiation topics included:

- Preservation of citywide/at-large representation.
- Timing and procedures for redistricting.
- Additional reforms, including campaign-finance discussions.

Their negotiations did not produce a single consolidated amendment.

The District 7 election illustrated the tension between district voters' preferences and a citywide electorate. It does not, by itself, establish whether the winning councilor would govern better or worse.

Similarly, research on district elections finds potential benefits for geographic and minority representation, but no universally optimal district size or guarantee of improved government performance.

Use these details only if they improve the infographic. Do not overcrowd the main design.

## Accuracy and editorial requirements

Before finalizing, verify all election-specific claims against official Columbus election materials, City Council records, and reliable local reporting.

In particular, verify:

- The operative language of Issues 8 and 9.
- The consequences if both pass.
- The timetable for adding seats under Issue 9.
- The scheduled redistricting dates.
- Whether either measure contains additional changes that require disclosure.
- The precise effect of each voting combination.

Additional changes reported in the proposals include district-representative recall procedures under Issue 8 and candidate-petition and population-deviation provisions under Issue 9.

If confirmed, include these in a discreet **“Other changes”** footnote or QR-linked expanded explainer. Do not bury material differences, but avoid cluttering the principal comparison.

If any fact in the supplied copy conflicts with authoritative election records, correct the copy before designing. Flag the correction in the deliverable notes rather than silently preserving inaccurate information.

Use a compact source footer with specific verified references and, where useful, a QR code linking to a fuller source list.

## Visual style

Use a modern, professional civic-information aesthetic.

Prioritize:

- Strong typography and generous whitespace.
- Large, instantly recognizable numbers: **9**, **13**, and **4**.
- Simple seat diagrams and minimal iconography.
- High contrast, accessible colors.
- Logical reading order for both mobile and print.
- Clear YES/NO labels.
- Balanced treatment of both proposals.
- Readability without zooming excessively.

Avoid:

- Party-associated red-versus-blue framing.
- Candidate portraits or campaign imagery.
- Political endorsements or advocacy language.
- Overly decorative backgrounds.
- Tiny, unreadable source text.
- Excessive text blocks.
- Visual framing that makes one proposal appear inherently preferable.
- Implied hierarchies between district and at-large members.

A restrained touch of humor is acceptable, but the infographic should remain suitable for civic education.

For example, cross-district infrastructure can be illustrated with a transit line crossing multiple district boundaries. Do not make the joke more prominent than the voting information.

## Deliverables

Create:

1. **Primary infographic:** A polished portrait-oriented graphic appropriate for digital sharing and printing.
2. **Mobile version:** Optimized for reading on a smartphone, potentially with adjusted spacing or layout.
3. **Editable source:** Preserve text, diagrams, labels, and other elements in an editable format rather than flattening the entire composition.
4. **Exported PNG:** High-resolution image suitable for direct sharing.
5. **Source notes:** A compact record of references, verified facts, and any corrections or qualifications made during production.

Use a consistent design system across all versions.

## Final quality review

Before delivery, assess the finished infographic against these questions:

- Can someone identify the principal difference between Issues 8 and 9 within five seconds?
- Can someone understand all four voting outcomes within fifteen seconds?
- Is the both-pass outcome unmistakably clear?
- Does the design accurately portray the difference between district and at-large seats?
- Are the proposals described with equivalent neutrality?
- Are the historical details factual rather than speculative?
- Does the infographic avoid equating electoral representation with government performance?
- Is all text legible at normal mobile viewing sizes?
- Can the design be edited without recreating it?
- Are the claims supported by verified sources?

**Success criterion:** A Columbus voter encountering this infographic should be able to understand the election mechanics, distinguish the two proposals, and make an informed independent decision without researching the underlying procedural history.

Proceed with design and production using this brief as the editorial and visual specification.
